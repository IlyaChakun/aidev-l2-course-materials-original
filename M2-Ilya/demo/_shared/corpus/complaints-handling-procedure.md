# Complaints Handling Procedure

This procedure describes how Lumen Support deals with complaints. It applies to every channel and every plan. The aim is that each customer who complains feels heard, receives a written answer from a responsible person and is not made to repeat their story.

## What is a complaint

A complaint is an expression of dissatisfaction about Lumen's product, service, communication, billing experience or the behaviour of a member of staff. It differs from a normal support question: the customer is not only asking for help, but is also saying that something went wrong or was unfair.

### Typical signs

- Words such as "complaint", "unacceptable", "disappointed", "I want to speak to a manager".
- A description of a bad experience with an earlier ticket.
- A statement that the customer will cancel, leave a negative review or contact a regulator because of how they were treated.
- A report about the conduct of a named or unnamed support agent.

### Complaint or bug report?

A report such as "the export button does nothing" is a technical problem, even if the customer is irritated. The same message with the added sentence "this is the third time it fails and nobody helps" contains a complaint. When a ticket has both a technical problem and a complaint, the complaint part is escalated, and the technical part is handled as usual if the documentation covers it.

### Tone is not a test

A sharp tone does not make a message a complaint, and a polite tone does not mean it is not one. The operator looks at the content: is the customer saying that Lumen or its staff did something wrong?

## First-line behaviour

The first line has three duties: **acknowledge, do not argue, do not defend**. After that, the ticket is escalated.

### Acknowledge

The operator thanks the customer for the feedback and shows that the message has been read by naming the concern in neutral words. The acknowledgement does not agree that Lumen was wrong and does not say it was right.

### Do not argue and do not defend

The operator does not explain why the customer is mistaken, does not list the steps that the team took, and does not defend the policy, the product or colleagues. Even a correct factual correction sounds like a contradiction to a customer who is upset. The correction can be made later by the Support Lead, with full information.

### Never judge the complaint

**The first line never judges whether a complaint is justified.** The operator does not write "this complaint is unfounded" or "you are right." Judgement is the task of the Support Lead, who sees the whole history.

### Acceptable and unacceptable wording

| Unacceptable | Acceptable |
|---|---|
| "That is not our fault." | "Thank you for telling us about this." |
| "You should have read the article." | "I understand that this has been frustrating." |
| "We cannot do anything about it." | "I have passed your message to the person who handles complaints." |
| "Our agent did nothing wrong." | "Your comment about the conversation will be reviewed." |

The operator also does not promise compensation, an apology on behalf of the company, or a particular result. Statements about refunds, credits or discounts are not made in a complaint reply; they belong to the Billing team and are described in separate documents.

## Escalation to the Support Lead

Every complaint is escalated to the **Support Lead**. The escalation contains a one-sentence factual reason and, where available, the key facts that the Support Lead needs.

### What the escalation contains

1. The reason in one short factual sentence, for example "Customer complains about the handling of an earlier ticket."
2. A reference to earlier tickets of the same customer, if the customer mentions them.
3. Any technical details already collected, such as the workspace name or the time in UTC.
4. The customer's stated wish, in neutral words, for example "Customer asks for a written explanation."

The reason must not include the operator's opinion. "Customer is unreasonable" is never an acceptable reason.

### What the customer is told

The customer is told that the complaint has been passed to the Support Lead and that they will receive a written reply. The operator mentions that the Support Lead replies within **2 business days**. Business days are Monday to Friday. The operator does not name a precise day or hour and does not state what the answer will be.

### Template

```text
Hello, thank you for telling us about this. I understand that this has been
frustrating. I have passed your message to our Support Lead, who handles
complaints. You will receive a written reply within 2 business days.
```

## Response by the Support Lead

The Support Lead reads the whole history, checks the facts and replies **in writing within 2 business days**. The written reply is sent to the customer through the same ticket, so that the conversation stays in one place.

### Content of the reply

A Support Lead's reply normally covers:

- A summary of what the customer reported, to show that it has been understood.
- The facts as established from the ticket history and, where relevant, other teams.
- What will change or what has been done, within the limits of documented policy.
- A contact path if the customer is not satisfied.

If the complaint touches billing or privacy, the Support Lead involves the Billing team or the Privacy team, and those teams make the decisions in their area. The Support Lead does not override their decisions.

### If the facts are not yet clear

When the investigation needs more time, the Support Lead sends a short written message within the 2 business days that explains that the review continues. The customer should not be left without information at the end of that period.

### Closing the complaint

A complaint is closed when the Support Lead's written reply has been sent and the customer has had a chance to respond. Closure is recorded in the ticket with a short note on the outcome. The Support Lead also records whether a pattern appears, for example repeated complaints about the same feature, so that the product and support teams can act on it.

## Complaints about an agent's conduct

Some complaints concern how a member of staff behaved: a rude reply, a dismissive tone, an inappropriate comment, or incorrect information given with confidence. These complaints are treated seriously and handled in a separate path.

### Routing

Complaints about conduct go to the **Quality team**, **via the Support Lead**. The first line does not send them to the Quality team directly. The operator escalates the ticket to the Support Lead in the usual way, and the Support Lead forwards it to the Quality team and informs the customer.

### Principles

| Principle | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Neutrality | The operator does not comment on the conduct that is being reported |
| Confidentiality | The operator does not discuss the complaint with the staff member concerned |
| Fairness | The Quality team hears both sides before reaching a conclusion |
| Written record | The outcome is documented in the ticket system |

If the complaint is about the operator's own earlier replies, the same rules apply. The operator does not try to correct the matter personally and does not remove or modify earlier messages.

## Repeated contact and priority

When a customer writes again about the same matter, the experience is getting worse for them. A message such as "this is the third time I write" is a clear signal that the earlier handling did not work.

### Priority rule

**Repeated contact raises the ticket priority to high.** The operator applies this rule when the customer says that they have already written several times, or when the ticket history shows earlier contacts about the same matter without a resolution. The priority levels themselves are defined in the escalation and routing policy.

### Examples

| Customer message | Handling |
|---|---|
| "Third time I write, nobody answers" | Priority high; escalate as a complaint |
| "I wrote yesterday about the same thing" | Check history; if unresolved, priority high |
| "Just following up on my question" | Normal follow-up; check history before deciding |

A single follow-up with a polite tone and a short interval is not automatically a complaint. The operator checks whether the target for the first response has already passed; the response targets are described in the support hours document.

### Avoiding more repetition

The operator links the new ticket to the earlier ones and never asks the customer to explain the problem from the beginning. The Support Lead receives the complete history.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can the first line apologise?** A neutral expression of understanding is acceptable, as in the table above. An apology that admits fault on behalf of Lumen is left to the Support Lead.

**What if the customer threatens to cancel?** The operator acknowledges the message and escalates. The operator does not offer discounts, credits or other incentives. Cancellation rules are in the refund and cancellation policy.

**What if the customer is abusive?** The operator stays neutral and polite, does not respond to insults and escalates the ticket to the Support Lead with a factual reason.

**Can the customer ask for a different agent?** The request is passed to the Support Lead with the complaint.

**Does a complaint change the plan response time?** No. The Support Lead's own written reply time of 2 business days applies to complaints regardless of plan.
