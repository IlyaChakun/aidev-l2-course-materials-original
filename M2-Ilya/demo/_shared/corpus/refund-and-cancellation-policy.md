# Refund and Cancellation Policy

This policy explains when Lumen can refund a payment, how a plan is cancelled, and what support staff may and may not say to customers who ask about money back. It applies to every paid workspace. Prices and plan limits are in the Plans and Pricing article, and the mechanics of invoices and payments are in the Billing, Invoices and Payments article.

The policy has two audiences. Customers use it to understand their options. First-line support staff use it to answer correctly without promising something that only the Billing team can decide.

## Principles

Three principles sit behind every rule in this document:

1. **Refunds are decided by one team.** Only the Billing team approves a refund. No other team, and no first-line agent, can approve, confirm or even predict one.
2. **The rules depend on the billing period.** Monthly plans and annual plans follow different rules, because an annual payment covers a much longer period.
3. **Honesty matters more than speed.** A customer who is told "you will probably get your money back" and is later refused is worse off than a customer who is told, correctly, that the case has been passed to the Billing team.

A refund is a return of money for a charge that was already paid. The following are not refunds and are handled elsewhere:

- A pending authorization hold on a card, which disappears on its own (see the Billing, Invoices and Payments article).
- A service credit after an outage, which is decided by the Billing team under the rules in the Service Status and Incidents article.
- A discount, which exists only through a signed order form (see the Enterprise Contracts and Discounts article).

Customers often use the word "refund" for all of these. The first step in every conversation is to understand which one the customer actually means.

## Monthly plans

**Monthly plans are not refundable.** When a monthly charge has been taken, the customer has paid for that month of access, and the amount is not returned when they stop using the workspace, forget to cancel in time, or decide the plan was not the right one.

There is one exception: a **confirmed billing error reported within 14 days**. A billing error means that Lumen charged something it should not have, for example:

- A charge for a plan or number of seats that the Owner never selected.
- A charge made after the plan was cancelled and the paid period had ended.
- A true duplicate charge, confirmed by the Billing team.

For the exception to apply, two conditions must both be true. The error must be reported to support within 14 days, and the Billing team must confirm that it really was an error. A customer who simply says "this was a mistake" has not yet established a billing error. The Billing team looks at the records and decides.

| Situation | Refundable? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Customer no longer needs the plan | No | Cancel before the next billing date |
| Customer forgot to cancel before renewal | No | Access continues until the paid period ends |
| Confirmed billing error, reported within 14 days | Possibly | Only the Billing team decides |
| Billing error reported after 14 days | Not under this policy | Escalate; the Billing team reviews the case |
| Authorization hold on the card | Not a charge | Disappears on its own |

Cancelling stops future charges but does not refund the month already paid. The workspace remains usable until the paid period ends.

## Annual plans

Annual plans have a **30-day money-back window from the first payment**. If the Owner asks for a refund within 30 days of the first payment for the annual plan, the request is eligible to be reviewed by the Billing team.

After 30 days:

- There are **no refunds**.
- There are **no prorated refunds**, so a customer who stops using Lumen after a few months does not receive money back for the unused months.
- The plan **runs until the end of the term**, and the workspace keeps all features until then.

The 30 days are counted from the date of the first payment, which is visible in the Billing area. They are not counted from the date of renewal. Renewal payments of an annual plan are not covered by the 30-day window. A customer who is surprised by a renewal charge should be told that it is a renewal and that the Billing team can review whether a billing error occurred, without any promise of money back.

### Moving from monthly to annual

Switching from a monthly plan to an annual plan creates a first payment for the annual plan. The 30-day window applies to that first annual payment. The months already paid on the monthly plan are not refunded.

### Moving between plans

Upgrades and downgrades are described in the Plans and Pricing article. A downgrade takes effect at the next renewal and does not create a refund for the difference.

## Who approves refunds and how long it takes

**Only the Billing team approves refunds.** They review the payment records, check the rules above and decide. The decision is not made by support agents, Account Managers, the Support Lead or the engineers.

When the Billing team approves a refund, it is **processed in 5-10 business days**. The time is counted from the approval, and the exact moment the money appears on the card statement also depends on the customer's bank.

This has two consequences for the first line:

1. **Never promise a refund.** Phrases such as "you will get your money back", "that should be refunded", or "this is eligible" are not allowed, even when the case looks obvious.
2. **Never confirm a refund.** Even when the customer says that someone from Lumen already promised one, the first line cannot confirm it. It escalates the ticket and lets the Billing team confirm it.

It is acceptable to describe the policy in general terms, for example, "annual plans have a 30-day money-back window from the first payment", and to say that the Billing team will review the request. It is not acceptable to apply the policy to the customer's case and announce the result.

### Refund timeline

| Step | Who | Typical outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Customer asks for a refund | Customer | Ticket created |
| First line collects the data and escalates | First line | Ticket moves to the Billing team |
| Review and decision | Billing team | Approved or declined |
| Processing | Billing team | 5-10 business days after approval |

## What the first line must collect

When a customer asks for a refund or reports a charge they do not recognise, the first line collects a short set of facts and then escalates. The data lets the Billing team find the payment without asking again.

Collect:

- The **payment reference** (`PAY-######`) shown in the invoice or in the receipt email.
- The **date** of the charge.
- The **amount** of the charge.
- The **last four digits of the card** only.

Never ask for, and never accept, a full card number, the CVC code, a password or a photo of the card. If a customer sends one, tell them not to send it again and do not copy it into the ticket.

An example of a short, correct escalation reason:

```
Customer asks for a refund of an annual plan; payment reference PAY-077305, charged on 2026-03-02, amount as on invoice, card ending 4417.
```

The reason is one factual sentence. It does not contain an opinion about the customer's right to a refund.

### Example reply to the customer

```
Hello, thank you for contacting us. I have passed your request to our Billing team together with the payment reference you sent. They review refund requests and will reply to you in this ticket. Please do not send any further card details.
```

The reply gives the next step, does not promise money back, and does not mention internal tools or internal rules. The style rules for all replies are in the Reply Style Guide.

## Cancelling a plan

The workspace **Owner** can cancel a plan. Nobody else can do it, including Admins.

1. Sign in as the Owner.
2. Open **Settings -> Billing -> Cancel plan**.
3. Read the summary of the date on which access will end.
4. Confirm the cancellation.

After cancellation, **access continues until the paid period ends**. A workspace that was paid for a month stays fully active until the end of that month, and an annual workspace stays active until the end of its term. Nothing is switched off immediately, and no further charge is made after the paid period.

### Export your data before cancelling

Before cancelling, the Owner should export any data they want to keep. Reports can be exported from the Reports page, and a full data export can be requested by the Owner. The steps, timing and conditions for a full export, and for deleting an account, are in the Data Privacy and GDPR Requests article. Cancelling a plan does not by itself delete the account or its data.

### Cancelling versus failing to pay

Cancelling is a deliberate action by the Owner. A failed payment is not a cancellation: the retry process in the Billing, Invoices and Payments article continues. If the customer wants to stop being charged, they should cancel in the Billing area and not just let payments fail.

## Special cases and escalation

Some requests do not fit neatly into the rules above. These cases always go to the Billing team, with the data listed earlier:

- A customer says they were charged after cancelling.
- A customer says they never created the workspace or did not authorise the payment.
- A customer reports two completed charges for the same period.
- A customer asks to be refunded after the 14-day or 30-day period has passed.
- A customer cites a statement from a Lumen employee that a refund was promised.
- A customer disputes the amount of a charge or an invoice.

If the customer threatens a bank chargeback or complains about how they were treated, acknowledge the message, do not argue, and also follow the Complaints Handling Procedure. A complaint about a refund decision goes to the Support Lead; the Billing team still owns the refund decision itself.

### Requests from someone who is not the Owner

A Member or Admin may write that the company wants a refund. Billing is managed only by the Owner, so the first line cannot treat this as a refund request from the account holder. The reply should explain that the Owner of the workspace must contact support from the Owner's own account.
