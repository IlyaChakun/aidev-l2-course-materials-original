# Have not chosen a tool yet

Then the most useful thing I can do is not to give you freedom of choice, but to say
it straight: take Claude Code together with your usual environment. Below I explain why,
and what to do after installing.

## Why exactly this

The reason is not that it is better than the others as an agent, that is debatable. The reason is
that I went through the whole path from installation to a log appearing in a remote repository with it
by hand, end to end, and all the commands in the instructions are copied from a terminal that worked.
The other setups either leave an unverified fork, or need a second tool,
or part of the work has to be done by hand.

A second argument: it works equally well in IntelliJ IDEA, in VS Code, and simply
in the terminal, because the plugin and the extension launch the same engine and write
to the same place. That is, if you change environment later, nothing has to be
reconfigured.

If you also do not have paid access yet, look at the separate material about access
and about connecting third-party models that I sent to the chat: it covers the options,
including free and inexpensive ones.

## What to do next

Install Claude Code, open your environment and then follow one of the two files next to this one.

If you are in IntelliJ IDEA, WebStorm, PyCharm or any other JetBrains IDE, open
`intellij-claude-code.md`.

If you are in VS Code, open `vscode-claude-code.md`.

If for now you work simply in the terminal, take the VS Code file: the commands there are the same,
only the section about checking the panel differs, which you will skip.

## What to do right away, without putting it off

Get to the first step of the chosen file and run the check that logs are being written, before
you start the homework. It takes ten seconds and answers a question that otherwise
surfaces at the moment of submission, when it is too late to fix.

And get into the habit of looking at your log with your own eyes before submitting. Not for security,
although for that too, but because it is the cheapest observation of your own work:
in your own conversation with the agent over a week you usually see more than in the finished code.

## If you change your mind

Submitting logs is voluntary and does not affect your grade, so if you decide not to take part,
nothing will happen. Still choose a tool: without an agent you will have nothing to do the homework with.
